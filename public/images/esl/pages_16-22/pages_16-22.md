It would seem that with a reasonably large set of training data, we could always approximate the theoretically optimal conditional expectation by k-nearest-neighbor averaging, since we should be able to find a fairly large neighborhood of observations close to any x and average them. This approach and our intuition breaks down in high dimensions, and the phenomenon is commonly referred to as the curse of dimensionality (Bellman, 1961). Consider the nearest-neighbor procedure for inputs uniformly distributed in a p-dimensional unit hypercube, as in Figure 2.6.
